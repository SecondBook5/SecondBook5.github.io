Scientific problem

What keeps tissue altered after the initiating injury?

Secondary lymphedema is not simply an acute inflammatory episode. Epithelial, stromal, immune, and lymphatic compartments remain remodeled, creating a useful system for studying persistent tissue state across multiple biological scales.

My role in the Mehrara laboratory is computational: I lead and contribute analyses that connect human single-cell transcriptomics, bulk RNA-seq, proteomics, and microbiome data to identify reproducible tissue programs and determine which apparent differences survive measurement-aware scrutiny.

Multi-omic strategy

Use each modality for the question it can actually answer.

Single-cell data localize state changes to specific cell populations. Bulk RNA-seq captures cohort-level transcriptional programs. Proteomics asks whether those programs are reflected at the protein level. Microbiome data add a measurement domain in which preprocessing and taxonomy can themselves create large apparent biological differences.

The point of combining modalities is not to maximize the number of assays in a figure. It is to ask whether a biological claim remains coherent when viewed through measurements with different failure modes.

Measurement lesson

A large biological-looking difference can disappear when the measurement process is harmonized.

In one cross-cohort microbiome analysis, related datasets initially appeared to differ by roughly two-fold in taxonomic diversity, approximately 210 versus 104 detected genera. After harmonizing classification, the counts were approximately 213 versus 203.

That discrepancy motivated Dysbioscope, a measurement-aware workflow for cross-cohort microbiome analysis. The lesson is broader than microbiome work: before explaining a striking difference biologically, test whether the analysis pipeline created it.

Public work

Separate the public record from ongoing analyses.

Published work from the collaboration has examined PAR2-associated epidermal remodeling and the broader omic architecture of secondary lymphedema. Ongoing analyses are described on this site only at a level appropriate for public disclosure.

This project is collaborative by design. The website therefore distinguishes my computational role from the experimental work performed by the Mehrara and Kataru teams.
