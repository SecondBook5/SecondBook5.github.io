Research

Inferring biological state from incomplete measurements

My work spans clinical imaging and molecular data, but the central problem is consistent: biological history is rarely observed directly. I develop and apply models that turn noisy measurements into testable representations of latent tissue, immune, and regulatory state.

Quantitative imaging · Host biology

HostBio / Comp-MRF

Using routine CT as a quantitative measurement of host tissue state

I conceived HostBio after recognizing that tissue-mixture problems in clinical imaging share useful structure with deconvolution problems I had encountered in spatial transcriptomics, while requiring a different measurement model. Comp-MRF treats partial-volume composition, spatial dependence, nuisance components, and within-scan reference tissues explicitly rather than reducing the thymus or other host compartments to a single thresholded volume.

Why the problem matters

Routine scans are abundant, longitudinal, and clinically embedded, but scanner settings, contrast, anatomy, and tissue mixing can obscure biology. The goal is not simply better segmentation. It is to recover phenotypes that remain interpretable enough to test against immune and clinical measurements.

What I developed

  • A scan-adaptive probabilistic tissue-composition framework for routine CT.
  • Partial-volume mixture modeling with spatial regularization, nuisance-component handling, and within-scan calibration.
  • A clinical-research implementation adopted across MSK through MIM Development.
  • Extensions from thymic composition to multi-organ host phenotyping across muscle, adipose, liver, adrenal, thyroid, and additional compartments.

How I test biological meaning

I integrate imaging-derived phenotypes with longitudinal flow cytometry, cytokine profiling, tumor genomics, treatment exposure, and outcomes. Across lung-cancer cohorts, this work has linked thymic involution to peripheral inhibitory-receptor phenotypes, treatment toxicity, disease control, and host immunometabolic states.

Spatial genomics · Regulatory dynamics

StageBridge

What can a static human tissue sample actually tell us about progression?

For my M.S. thesis in Bioinformatics at Johns Hopkins University, I developed StageBridge to study premalignant epithelial progression from cross-sectional spatial and single-nucleus transcriptomic data. Rather than treating progression as a generic pseudotime problem, I represent epithelial regulatory state together with local immune, stromal, extracellular-matrix, inflammatory, and spatial context.

Regulatory representation

The framework operates on transcription-factor and pathway activities and gene regulatory structure rather than relying only on raw expression coordinates. This makes the latent space more biologically interpretable and lets the model ask whether tissue context is associated with different regulatory transitions.

Model criticism became part of the method

An early context-neutral transport assumption looked plausible in an abstract latent benchmark but failed after projection into biological regulatory space. Prespecified recoverability testing dropped to a Tier-2 cosine of 0.27. I redesigned the coupling using conditional stratification, improving Tier-2 recovery to 0.84, and narrowed the estimand accordingly. The point was not to rescue a preferred model at any cost. It was to determine which claim the data could actually support.

Current interpretation

StageBridge is therefore framed as conditional regulatory transport from sparse cross-sectional observations, not as reconstruction of an individual's causal trajectory. I evaluate robustness with patient-held-out analyses, matched-context shuffles, degeneracy controls, and cross-disease testing.

Multi-omics · Chronic inflammation

Secondary lymphedema

Persistent tissue remodeling after inflammatory injury

In collaboration with the Mehrara laboratory at Memorial Sloan Kettering, I study how epithelial, stromal, immune, and lymphatic compartments remain altered after chronic inflammatory injury in secondary lymphedema.

Computational scope

  • Paired-donor single-cell transcriptomics of normal and lymphedematous human skin.
  • Bulk RNA-seq of lymphatic injury and repair.
  • Label-free proteomics of healthy and diseased tissue.
  • Cross-cohort microbiome analysis with measurement-aware harmonization.

Why this work matters to my broader research program

Lymphedema gives me a system in which biological memory is visible at multiple scales. The disease is not simply an acute inflammatory state. Tissue compartments remain remodeled after injury, raising the question of which regulatory programs stabilize those states and which measurements can distinguish persistent biology from technical or compositional artifacts.

Public work

Published studies have examined PAR2-associated epidermal remodeling and the broader omic architecture of secondary lymphedema. Ongoing analyses are described here only at the level that is already appropriate for public disclosure.
