Computational & Systems Biology · Memorial Sloan Kettering

I study how biological systems record experience, and how those histories reshape future cell and tissue states.

I am a Research Assistant at Memorial Sloan Kettering working across quantitative clinical imaging, radiobiology, systems immunology, regulatory biology, and single-cell and spatial genomics. I build interpretable computational models for latent biological states that must be inferred from sparse, heterogeneous observations.

Imaging host biology

HostBio / Comp-MRF

Routine clinical scans contain information about host tissue state that is usually reduced to anatomy. I conceived HostBio to recover interpretable tissue-composition phenotypes from CT despite acquisition variability and partial-volume mixing, then connect those phenotypes to immune state, genomics, treatment response, toxicity, and outcome.

Regulatory dynamics

StageBridge

My M.S. thesis at Johns Hopkins asks what aspects of progression can be inferred when human tissue is observed only cross-sectionally. StageBridge combines spatial and single-nucleus transcriptomics, transcription factor activity, gene regulatory networks, and context-conditioned transport to study how local tissue context shapes regulatory state transitions.

Research approach

Across modalities, I keep returning to three questions.

What is the latent biological state?

How can it be inferred from noisy, incomplete, indirect measurements without confusing the measurement process for the biology?

What structure can transfer?

Which computational ideas remain useful across imaging, spatial genomics, and other modalities, and where does the analogy stop being valid?

What is actually identifiable?

Which conclusions are constrained by the observations, and which require stronger assumptions, orthogonal measurements, or experimental validation?
