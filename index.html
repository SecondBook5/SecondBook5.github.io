Computational & Systems Biology · Memorial Sloan Kettering

I study how inflammation, aging, and treatment leave biological memory in tissues, and how those histories reshape future cell and immune states.

I build interpretable computational models that recover latent biological state from routine imaging, single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, and other incomplete human measurements, with particular attention to measurement physics, uncertainty, identifiability, and biological validation.

Imaging host biology

HostBio / Comp-MRF

I conceived and engineered HostBio to turn routine CT into an interpretable measurement of host tissue state rather than a purely anatomical image. Comp-MRF models partial-volume composition, spatial dependence, nuisance components, and within-scan references, and the resulting framework is now implemented for MSK clinical research through MIM Development.

I use those phenotypes to test links between host composition, immune state, tumor genomics, treatment response, toxicity, and outcome across lung-cancer cohorts.

Regulatory dynamics

StageBridge

My M.S. thesis asks what aspects of biological progression can actually be inferred when human tissue is observed only cross-sectionally. I developed StageBridge around spatial and single-nucleus transcriptomics, transcription-factor activity, gene regulatory networks, and context-conditioned transport.

When an initially plausible transport assumption failed in biological regulatory space, I redesigned the coupling and narrowed the claim rather than preserve an unsupported trajectory interpretation.

Persistent inflammatory state

Secondary lymphedema multi-omics

In collaboration with the Mehrara laboratory, I use human single-cell and bulk transcriptomics, proteomics, and microbiome data to study why epithelial, stromal, immune, and lymphatic compartments remain altered after chronic lymphatic injury.

This work provides a complementary system for asking when persistent molecular signatures reflect durable tissue-state biology and when they instead arise from composition, cohort structure, or measurement artifacts.

How I approach problems

The model is useful only if the inference is defensible.

What is the latent biological state?

How can it be recovered from noisy, indirect measurements without confusing the measurement process for the biology itself?

What structure can transfer?

Which computational ideas remain useful across imaging, spatial genomics, and other modalities, and what must change when the measurement physics changes?

What is actually identifiable?

Which conclusions are constrained by the observations, and which require stronger assumptions, orthogonal measurements, or experimental perturbation?
