Research Assistant · Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center

I study how inflammatory, age-associated, and treatment history reshape immune and tissue states, and how those changes can be inferred from incomplete human measurements.

Across quantitative imaging, radiobiology, single-cell and spatial genomics, and regulatory modeling, I develop interpretable computational approaches that make measurement assumptions, uncertainty, and identifiability explicit.

Method development · MSK

HostBio / Comp-MRF

I conceived and engineered HostBio to turn routine CT into an interpretable measurement of host tissue state rather than a purely anatomical image. Comp-MRF models partial-volume composition, spatial dependence, nuisance components, and within-scan references, and is implemented for MSK clinical research through MIM Development.

I then test whether those imaging-derived phenotypes correspond to biology outside the image using immune profiling, tumor genomics, treatment context, toxicity, response, and outcomes.

Quantitative CT · Probabilistic modeling · Systems immunology

Master's thesis · Johns Hopkins

StageBridge

StageBridge asks what aspects of biological progression can actually be inferred when human tissue is observed only cross-sectionally. I developed it around spatial and single-nucleus transcriptomics, transcription-factor activity, gene regulatory networks, and context-conditioned transport.

When an initially plausible transport assumption failed in biological regulatory space, I redesigned the coupling and narrowed the claim rather than preserve an unsupported trajectory interpretation.

Spatial transcriptomics · Regulatory networks · Optimal transport

Collaborative systems biology · MSK

Secondary lymphedema

In collaboration with the Mehrara laboratory, I use human single-cell and bulk transcriptomics, proteomics, and microbiome data to study why epithelial, stromal, immune, and lymphatic compartments remain altered after chronic lymphatic injury.

The project is also a test bed for measurement-aware analysis: persistent molecular signatures are only useful if they survive compositional, cohort, and pipeline artifacts.

Single-cell RNA-seq · Proteomics · Multi-omics

Research approach

The model is useful only if the inference is defensible.

01

Separate biology from measurement.

Ask how the observation was generated before interpreting the signal. Imaging intensity, transcript counts, and taxonomic profiles each distort biology differently.

02

Transfer structure carefully.

Reuse mathematical ideas across modalities when the underlying inverse problem is shared, but rebuild the measurement model when the physics or biology changes.

03

Make limits part of the result.

Test what is identifiable, stress assumptions, and narrow the claim when the data do not support the stronger interpretation.
