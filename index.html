Method development · MSK

HostBio / Comp-MRF

I conceived and engineered HostBio to turn routine CT into an interpretable measurement of host tissue composition. Comp-MRF explicitly models partial-volume mixing, spatial dependence, nuisance components, and within-scan references rather than reducing the problem to thresholding or segmentation.

The framework is implemented for MSK clinical research through MIM Development and has been validated across 1,078 patients in four independent NSCLC cohorts, including randomized RTOG-0617, using immune, genomic, treatment, toxicity, response, and survival data.

Master's thesis · Johns Hopkins

StageBridge

StageBridge asks what aspects of progression can actually be inferred when human tissue is observed only cross-sectionally. I developed it around spatial and single-nucleus transcriptomics, transcription-factor activity, gene regulatory networks, and context-conditioned transport.

A hidden recoverability failure exposed an initially plausible transport assumption. Reformulating the model around conditional transport improved transition recoverability from 0.27 to 0.84, while external PanIN testing returned a calibrated null where the data did not support reliable transition structure.

Recoverability 0.27 → 0.84 · donor-held-out testing · calibrated external null

Collaborative systems biology · MSK

Secondary lymphedema

In collaboration with the Mehrara laboratory, I use human single-cell and bulk transcriptomics, proteomics, microbiome data, and orthogonal experimental measurements to study persistent epithelial, stromal, immune, and lymphatic remodeling after chronic injury.

The same work has become a test bed for measurement-aware analysis. Across more than 500 microbiome samples, harmonized classification showed that an apparent nearly two-fold richness difference was largely created by incompatible taxonomic processing rather than biology.

State-aware single-cell analysis · >500 microbiome samples · multimodal validation

Research approach

The model is useful only if the inference is defensible.

01

Separate biology from measurement.

Ask how the observation was generated before interpreting the signal. Imaging intensity, transcript counts, and taxonomic profiles each distort biology differently.

02

Transfer structure carefully.

Reuse mathematical ideas across modalities when the underlying inverse problem is shared, but rebuild the measurement model when the physics or biology changes.

03

Make limits part of the result.

Test what is identifiable, stress assumptions, and narrow the claim when the data do not support the stronger interpretation.
