Research Experience

Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center

Research Assistant, Advanced Computing and Oncology Laboratory · PI: Tafadzwa L. Chaunzwa, MD

Aug 2026–Present

Graduate Research Assistant, Mar 2025–Jul 2026

  • Conceived and engineered HostBio (Comp-MRF), an interpretable, scan-adaptive probabilistic framework for recovering host-tissue composition from routine CT; translated mixture-deconvolution ideas from spatial transcriptomics into quantitative imaging and developed the method into a tool implemented for clinical research at MSK through MIM Development.
  • Validated and generalized HostBio across independent lung-cancer cohorts, linking imaging-derived host phenotypes with immune state, tumor genomics, treatment exposures, toxicity, response, and outcome, and extending the framework from thymic composition to multi-organ host phenotyping.
  • Applied AI and deep learning to thoracic imaging, supporting training and deployment of an nnU-Net v2 thymus-segmentation model and evaluating the pretrained Sybil model for CT-based lung-cancer risk prediction in female never-smokers; predicted risk significantly separated cancer cases from controls across all six prediction horizons in a 77-patient analysis.
  • Developed longitudinal quantitative-imaging approaches for serial cone-beam CT and tumor trajectories using radiomics, morphology, deformation analysis, mixed-effects models, joint longitudinal-survival modeling, and dynamic prediction; contributed the MSK clinical-imaging analysis to a collaborative mechanistic-modeling and machine-learning study of radiation-induced tumor migration.
  • Engineered reusable computational infrastructure for multimodal oncology, including COSMIC-IMPACT for integrated MSK-IMPACT tumor sequencing and MSK-ACCESS longitudinal ctDNA analysis; established reproducible laboratory workflows, trained researchers, and developed a departmental research-analytics platform at the request of the Chair of Radiation Oncology.
Additional project detail
  • Extended quantitative host phenotyping across thymus, skeletal muscle, adipose tissue, liver, adrenal, thyroid, and additional host compartments to study coordinated tissue-composition states rather than isolated organ-size measures.
  • Integrated CT-derived thymic composition with longitudinal flow cytometry, cytokine profiling, tumor genomics, treatment exposures, and outcomes in locally advanced NSCLC, including an 89-patient cohort contributing 175 pretreatment blood samples.
  • Defined a host immunometabolic phenotype in 73 patients with resectable NSCLC by integrating thymic activity and myosteatosis with PD-L1, tumor genomics, chromosomal instability, toxicity, and pathologic response.
  • Developed reusable longitudinal-imaging pipelines spanning radiomics, tumor geometry, deformation fields, and patient-level trajectories, including analyses of the CURB cohort.
  • Built shared data organization, provenance, automation, and reproducibility infrastructure for the laboratory and trained researchers in quantitative imaging and computational oncology workflows.

Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center

Computational Research Collaborator, Mehrara Laboratory · PI: Babak J. Mehrara, MD

Nov 2025–Present

  • Analyzed paired-donor single-cell transcriptomic data from normal and lymphedematous human skin to characterize mast-cell and inflammatory-state remodeling and integrate cellular findings with orthogonal experimental evidence.

Johns Hopkins University

Master's Thesis Research, StageBridge / CRRT-UDE · Advisor: Christopher Bradburne, PhD

2025–2026
  • Conceived and developed StageBridge, a computational framework for inferring context-conditioned premalignant epithelial progression from cross-sectional spatial and single-nucleus transcriptomic data by coupling epithelial regulatory state with local immune, stromal, extracellular-matrix, inflammatory, and spatial context.
  • Formulated Context-Residual Regulatory Transport via Universal Differential Equations (CRRT-UDE) to separate intrinsic regulatory progression from niche-associated residual dynamics across transcription-factor and pathway activity space while representing uncertainty and limits on inference from cross-sectional observations.
  • Diagnosed failure of an original context-neutral optimal-transport assumption using prespecified synthetic recoverability benchmarks; biological regulatory projection reduced Tier-2 recovery to cosine 0.27, while conditional stratification improved recovery to 0.84 and supported a narrower conditional-transport interpretation.
  • Evaluated robustness with patient-held-out analysis, matched-context shuffles, degeneracy controls, and cross-disease testing; presented StageBridge at the 2026 Center for Tumor Immune Systems Biology Symposium at Sloan Kettering Institute.
